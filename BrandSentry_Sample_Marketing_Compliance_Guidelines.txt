BRANDSENTRY AI — SAMPLE MARKETING COMPLIANCE GUIDELINES Document ID:
BSAI-DEMO-001 Version: 1.0 Purpose: Demonstration guideline for testing
evidence-grounded marketing claim review. Important: This is a fictional
sample policy created for software demonstration, not legal advice.

1.  GUARANTEES AND ABSOLUTE CLAIMS Marketing materials must not use the
    phrase “100% guaranteed” unless the Legal team has provided written
    approval. Marketing materials must not promise guaranteed outcomes
    when results depend on customer circumstances. Claims using words
    such as “always”, “never”, or “zero risk” must be supported by
    documented evidence and approved before publication.

2.  SAFETY CLAIMS Marketing materials must not describe a product as
    “100% safe”. Safety statements must be limited to risks that have
    been assessed and documented. Do not imply that a product has no
    risks unless the claim has been substantiated and approved by Legal.

3.  HEALTH AND MEDICAL CLAIMS Marketing materials must not claim that a
    product cures, treats, or prevents a disease unless the claim has
    been reviewed and approved through the applicable medical and
    regulatory process. Do not imply that a product replaces
    professional medical advice, diagnosis, or treatment. Health-related
    performance claims must be supported by appropriate evidence and
    reviewed before publication.

4.  PERFORMANCE AND COMPARATIVE CLAIMS Claims about performance, speed,
    savings, or effectiveness must be supported by current, reproducible
    evidence. Comparisons with competitors must use fair, verifiable,
    and up-to-date information. Do not state a specific percentage
    improvement unless the underlying measurement and methodology are
    documented.

5.  CUSTOMER TESTIMONIALS Testimonials must reflect genuine customer
    experiences and must not be edited in a way that changes their
    meaning. Do not imply that an exceptional result is typical unless
    representative evidence supports that implication. Any required
    disclosure about incentives or material relationships must be
    included.

6.  PRICING AND DISCOUNTS Advertised prices and discounts must be
    accurate and available under the stated conditions. A discount must
    not be described as “the lowest price ever” unless historical
    pricing records substantiate the claim. Material limitations, fees,
    and eligibility conditions must be clearly disclosed.

7.  ENVIRONMENTAL CLAIMS Environmental claims such as “eco-friendly”,
    “green”, or “zero impact” must be specific and supported by
    documented evidence. Avoid broad environmental claims when only a
    limited product feature has been assessed. Do not claim a product is
    carbon neutral unless the basis for that claim is documented and
    approved.

8.  DATA, PRIVACY, AND SECURITY CLAIMS Claims that customer data is
    “completely secure” or “impossible to hack” must not be used.
    Security and privacy statements must accurately reflect verified
    controls and documented practices. Do not claim regulatory
    certification or compliance unless the relevant status has been
    verified.

9.  EVIDENCE AND APPROVAL Every material factual marketing claim must
    have a traceable evidence source. Evidence must be current, relevant
    to the exact claim, and retained for review. Claims that are
    unsupported or ambiguous must be escalated for verification rather
    than presented as established fact. Where this guideline does not
    address a claim, the reviewer must record that the available
    guideline provides insufficient evidence.

10. REVIEW OUTCOMES SUPPORTED: The available guideline evidence
    affirmatively supports the claim. CONTRADICTED: The available
    guideline evidence directly conflicts with the claim. INSUFFICIENT
    EVIDENCE: The available guideline evidence does not establish
    whether the claim complies. A semantic similarity score alone is not
    proof that a claim is supported or contradicted. Reviewers must cite
    the actual source passage and page where available. They must not
    invent quotations, page numbers, or policy requirements.

END OF SAMPLE GUIDELINE
